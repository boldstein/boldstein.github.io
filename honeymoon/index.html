Phase 1 · The Appetizer

Tokyo

4 NIGHTS
4 Nights

With that first night behind us, we can actually start exploring. Four nights is enough to range across the whole city instead of picking a side, and Shinjuku sits right on the Yamanote Line loop, so both directions are equally easy from home base. West side: vintage shopping in Shimokitazawa, good coffee, and Harajuku and Shibuya whenever we're up for the crowds. East side: old Tokyo in Asakusa, upscale wandering in Ginza, and browsing Akihabara if we're in the mood for it.

  • Glitch Coffee & RoastersCoffee Shop
    A well-known third-wave coffee roaster in Kanda, popular with serious coffee people.
  • Bear Pond EspressoCoffee Shop
    A well-known Shimokitazawa espresso bar, run by one intense, exacting barista. Better than the older, smokier kissaten cafes nearby.
  • ShimokitazawaNeighborhood
    A neighborhood known for vintage clothing shops, live music venues, and narrow, winding streets.
  • Tokyo Metro Government BuildingObservation Deck
    Free observation deck, 202 meters up, with Mount Fuji visible on a clear day.
  • Meikyoku Kissa LionJazz Kissaten
    A listening bar in Shibuya running since 1926. You sit in silence and listen to classical and jazz records on a huge vintage sound system, no talking allowed.
  • KoenjiNeighborhood
    A scrappier, less-touristed neighborhood known for tiny record shops, punk bars, and hole-in-the-wall live-music venues.
  • Shinbashi YokochoFood Alley
    A narrow alley of small bars and food stalls, tucked under the train tracks. This is the classic after-work spot where Tokyo's salarymen go to drink.
  • Senso-ji & AsakusaOld Tokyo
    Tokyo's oldest temple, with the Nakamise shopping street leading up to it. The old-downtown counterpart to the west side's modern sprawl.
  • GinzaUpscale District
    Tokyo's high-end shopping and architecture district, flagship stores and department store basement food halls.
  • teamLabImmersive Art
    A large-scale digital art museum, rooms of projection-mapped, interactive installations. Worth checking which of the two locations (Planets or Borderless) is currently open.

Nanten-en

2 NIGHTS
2 Nights · Seiryuu-tei

A 1913 ryokan in Kawachinagano, on the Osaka Prefecture side, designed by Kingo Tatsuno, the same architect behind Tokyo Station. We booked the Seiryuu-tei room, the one suite here with its own open-air private onsen. Two nights, nowhere to be, Washoku Kaiseki dinner and breakfast both nights. Still confirming a vegan version of the kaiseki with the property directly.

  • Seiryuu-teiPrivate Onsen Suite
    The only room at Nanten-en with its own open-air hot spring bath. Other rooms share communal baths.
  • Washoku KaisekiMulti-Course Dinner
    Traditional Japanese haute cuisine, served course by course. Vegan adaptation requested, pending confirmation.
  • Kingo TatsunoArchitect
    Designed Tokyo Station and the Bank of Japan's head office. Nanten-en is a registered Cultural Property from the same era.

Koyasan

1 NIGHT
1 Night · Temple Stay

A quiet mountain reset after two nights of onsen soaking. The food is vegan by tradition, mornings include a meditation session, and we're doing a night tour through a 1,200-year-old cemetery.

  • ShukuboTemple Lodging
    An overnight stay inside a working Buddhist temple, meals and morning rituals included.

    Traditional Buddhist temple cooking. No meat, fish, or dashi, it's been vegan for centuries.

    Japan's largest cemetery, a forested path lined with over 200,000 lanterns and old stone markers. Especially atmospheric at night.

Cut, on purpose

The Cutting Room Floor

A few places we considered and decided against, and why.

Osaka City

Why cut

It's visually similar to Tokyo, and its street food relies heavily on octopus, pork, and dashi, hard to eat spontaneously as vegans. We used the extra time for a more relaxed stay in Tokyo instead. (Nanten-en is technically in Osaka Prefecture, but it's a rural onsen town, not the city itself, we're still skipping Dotonbori and Osaka Castle this trip.)

Art Islands (Naoshima / Teshima)

Why cut

It takes 2 to 3 hours of transit plus a ferry, and needs at least 2 nights, which would have meant cutting Kanazawa or Koyasan. Vegan food is also limited there.

Hokkaido (Noboribetsu)

Why cut

Too far north, would require a domestic flight.

A Fancy Tokyo Hotel Finale

Why cut

We originally planned a few nights back in Tokyo at a luxury hotel (Park Hyatt Tokyo, New York Bar and all) to close out the trip. Once the Mount Fuji stop came together, the nights had to go somewhere, and this got traded for them. Still on the list for a future trip.

Hakone

Why cut

The famous, polished private-onsen destination, and Motoyu Kansuiro's vegan kaiseki was genuinely tempting. But once Nanten-en's Seiryuu-tei room came together, keeping both an Osaka-side onsen and a Hakone-side onsen felt like two versions of the same idea. The Hiramatsu Sengokuhara stays on the list as a backup if Nanten-en ever falls through.

Kawaguchiko & Mount Fuji

Why cut

Originally the trip's onsen-with-a-Fuji-view stop. Once we started weighing vegan kaiseki options against Ari's ryokan research, Nanten-en outside Osaka won out on food and on being nearly on the way to Koyasan already. That meant no more reason to build a whole leg out toward Fuji, so the hiking, Chureito Pagoda, and lake views are cut for this trip. Worth revisiting on a future one.

Vegan Minshuku Sanbiki Neko

Why cut

100% vegan facility, but breakfast-only, no dinner, no private onsen, and reads more "vegan B&B" than the ryokan experience we're after.
